Reusable Welding Jig
Vega Street Bike Chassis

SolidWorksSheet MetalLaser CuttingChassis Assembly

Designed and developed a reusable welding jig to support the accurate assembly of the Vega Street Bike chassis during fabrication. The jig was manufactured using sheet metal base plates and mounting brackets, with box section bars forming the main structural skeleton. All components were cut, drilled, and welded to SolidWorks manufacturing drawings. Precise laser-level alignment across all axes was performed before any chassis components were introduced, guaranteeing all locating points were true to design geometry.

  • Sheet metal and box section construction — laser cut and MIG welded to drawing
  • Laser-level calibration across all axes prior to chassis introduction
  • Ensured precise chassis dimensional accuracy and minimised weld distortion
  • Reusable, modular design enabling multiple build cycles
  • Full SolidWorks 3D CAD model with manufacturing drawings

Rear Paddock Stand
Vega Street Bike

SolidWorksFEAMild SteelMIG Welding

Designed and developed a rear paddock stand for the Vega Street Bike, enabling safe rear wheel elevation during maintenance and servicing. Concept sketches were evaluated for material efficiency, manufacturability, and user safety. The selected design was fully modelled in SolidWorks and structurally validated with FEA in SolidWorks Simulation. Fabrication used mild steel hollow sections cut to drawing, positioned with a dedicated welding jig, MIG welded, and surface finished with primer and paint.

  • Von Mises stress analysis of stand frame under applied hand force
  • Von Mises stress analysis of assembly under motorcycle rear weight loading
  • Resultant displacement (URES) analysis of the connecting mount
  • Fabricated in mild steel hollow sections — laser cut, jig-positioned, MIG welded
  • Primer and paint finish applied; tested on the actual Vega Street Bike

Quick Jack
Falcon E2 Race Car

SolidWorksStructural DesignRace CarPit Stop

Designed a quick jack mechanism for the Falcon E2 Formula Student race car to enable rapid wheel and undercarriage access during pit stops. The design prioritised deployment speed, structural strength under dynamic loads, and compact packaging suited to the tight spatial constraints of the Formula Student vehicle. Full SolidWorks 3D CAD modelling was completed with manufacturing drawings for team fabrication.

  • Rapid-deployment mechanism designed for minimum pit-stop cycle time

Steering System Redesign
Falcon E2 Race Car

SolidWorksStructural RedesignBearing AlignmentRace Car

The previous season's cantilevered steering column mount flexed noticeably under steering loads, causing excessive play and loss of driver precision. The mount was completely redesigned with a triangular structure distributing loads across multiple chassis contact points, supplemented by a bent pipe interconnecting the two mounting points to prevent independent deflection. A dedicated laser-aligned bearing collar jig was designed and used during fabrication to ensure perfect coaxial alignment of both steering column bearings before and during welding.

  • Cantilevered mount replaced with triangulated structure — eliminated steering flex
  • Bent pipe interconnection between mounting points improves overall rigidity
  • Bearing collar alignment jig designed for coaxial bearing positioning during welding
  • Laser alignment tool used to confirm jig accuracy prior to welding
  • Resulted in binding-free, low-friction steering shaft with minimal play
