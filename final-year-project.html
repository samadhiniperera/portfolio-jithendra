Hydrodynamic Tunnel Design & Development

Design and Development of a Hydrodynamic Tunnel
for Experimental Fluid Dynamics

Final Year ProjectANSYS Fluent CFDMATLABFEAGlass Fibre Fabrication

Hydrodynamic tunnels are used across marine engineering, hydrofoil testing, high-Reynolds-number research and CFD model validation — yet Sri Lanka had no purpose-built facility of its own, forcing costly overseas testing and leaving local CFD-validation needs unmet. This project designed, simulated, and physically fabricated a small/medium-scale, vertical, closed-loop hydrodynamic tunnel with an open-channel test section, built to generate steady, uniform, low-turbulence flow for external flow experiments. The core experimental goal was reproducing the full range of flow regimes around a cylindrical body — from laminar steady flow through to transitional unsteady flow — across Reynolds numbers from 0 to roughly 8,000, based on cylinder diameter, inside a 150 × 150 mm test section at a flow rate of 0.0136 m³/s.

The design followed a four-phase process: configuration selection grounded in literature review, analytical design and sizing of every component, CFD and FEA optimisation, and finally fabrication and assembly. A MATLAB head-loss model confirmed the full circuit's head loss (~0.9 m) sits comfortably within the available pump head (9.14 m), showing head loss alone couldn't be used to optimise the design — so single-phase ANSYS Fluent CFD was used instead. Transient CFD of the 35° wide-angle diffuser revealed severe flow separation; four guide vanes (7° per channel, following the Waitman–Reneau–Kline method) were introduced and cut turbulence intensity through the diffuser from ~10.7% down to 2.08%, while achieving a pressure recovery coefficient (Cₚ) of 0.593. FEA on the glass-fibre components showed that an unreinforced 3 mm GFRP wall exceeded the 60 MPa allowable stress, so external ribs and additional GFRP layers were added across all structural parts.

Every primary component — the vertical transition, 90° bends, the wide-angle diffuser with integrated splitter vanes, the settling chamber, the contraction section, the test section, and the honeycomb flow straightener — was hand-laid in glass-fibre-reinforced plastic (GFRP) on glass moulds for a smooth internal finish, paired with a low-cost 3D-printed PLA honeycomb. The completed tunnel was fully assembled, commissioned, and validated using dye flow visualisation around a test cylinder, making it Sri Lanka's first hydrodynamic tunnel with a consolidated, replicable design framework for future facilities built under similar budget, space, and material constraints.

  • Sri Lanka's first purpose-built hydrodynamic tunnel — designed, fabricated, and commissioned end-to-end
  • Vertical closed-loop circuit, open-channel test section, targeting Re 0–8,000 around a cylinder
  • MATLAB head-loss model verified pump adequacy — ~0.9 m loss vs 9.14 m available head
  • ANSYS Fluent CFD — 4-vane splitter design cut diffuser turbulence intensity from ~10.7% to 2.08% (Cₚ = 0.593)
  • FEA-driven redesign of GFRP wall thickness, ribs, and reinforcement across all structural components
  • Full glass-fibre fabrication by hand layup, plus a 3D-printed PLA honeycomb flow straightener
  • Commissioned and validated with dye flow visualisation around a test cylinder
